The brief

A founder with a clear idea, and nothing built yet

Terroo is a free platform where travelers meet locals for real encounters: a shared meal, a neighbourhood walk, an evening out. No listings, no fees, no guided tour.

I came onto the project before there was a single screen designed. The founder had the concept, the name, and a strong instinct for what Terroo shouldn't feel like: not Couchsurfing's dated forum aesthetic, not Airbnb Experiences' transactional listings page. My job was to give the idea a face it didn't have yet, working directly from the founder's vision rather than an existing brand to extend. Everything from the type pairing to the button shadows came out of that blank-page conversation.

Art direction

Four decisions that built the brand

01 A serif with some grit

Most travel startups reach for a clean grotesque and call it done. I paired Fraunces, a serif with a slightly rough, hand-set quality, against Inter Tight for body copy. The serif carries the brand's voice; the sans keeps everything else legible.

Travel through people.

Fraunces / Inter Tight

02 Rust, not travel-app blue

Every booking app defaults to blue. I built the palette around a sun-baked rust on a paper-like cream, closer to a passport stamp than a SaaS dashboard. It reads as warm and analog before a single word is read.

03 Photos pinned like postcards

I was tempted to lay the experience photos out in a clean, even grid, the way every other travel site does. That felt like it belonged to the same category of app Terroo is trying not to be. Pinning each photo like a postcard, tape and all, makes the encounters feel handed to you, not served by an algorithm.

04 Stamped, not flat, buttons

Every pill button carries a second, darker shape offset behind it, like a stamp pressed slightly off-register. It's a small detail that shows up on every CTA on the site, and it's the detail that made the whole brand feel tactile instead of templated.
