The brief

A working product that didn't yet look trustworthy

Yogspace connects students with certified yoga teachers for live, 1:1 classes taught in or inspired by traditional Indian lineages. This wasn't a blank page: the founder had already built and shipped it.

What Yogspace was selling was credibility: a verified certificate, a real lineage, a teacher worth paying for over a free YouTube video. The site didn't say any of that visually. Rounded pill shapes, a chunky display font, flat cream blocks with no depth, it read closer to a kids' app than a platform asking people to trust a stranger with their practice. I wasn't inventing a brand from nothing here. I was closing the gap between what the product actually did and what it looked like it did.

Art direction

Four decisions behind the redesign

01 An editorial serif, not a rounded mascot font

The original type was a chunky, rounded display face that made a certification platform read as playful. I moved headlines to EB Garamond, paired with Karla for UI text, closer to a hospitality brand than a fitness app.

Learn yoga from its source.

EB Garamond / Karla

02 Away from the wellness-teal cliché

Every yoga app reaches for the same sage green or spa teal. I chose a warm terracotta instead, closer to saffron, a quiet nod to the Indian flag and the lineage these teachers actually trained in. Set against cream and a near-black navy for UI weight, it reads closer to a considered travel or hospitality brand than a template.

03 Trust made visible, not just stated

"Certified" meant nothing as a plain word in a paragraph. I designed a small set of custom badge icons (top rated, certified, specialist) and turned each teacher's certification history into an actual timeline, so the credential is something you can actually see.

04 Fewer pills, more air

I wanted these pages to feel soft and to breathe, the same two things a yoga teacher asks of you on the mat. Soft meant dropping hard borders in favor of warm-toned shadows that separate containers without a hard edge. Breathing meant fewer, larger blocks and wide open spacing instead of the original's dense run of identical pill shapes. Both exist to reassure more than to decorate: a calmer surface is easier to trust.
